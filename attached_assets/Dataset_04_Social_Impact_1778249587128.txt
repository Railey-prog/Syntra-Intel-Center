Source: Issues in Information Systems, Volume 26, Issue 4, pp. 322-331, 2025 (Experimental study on AI-generated misinformation on Instagram/Meta, Feb 12 - April 20, 2025)
Tags: AI-generated content, social media misinformation, disinformation, fake content, automation, human oversight, Meta, Instagram, engagement farming, content creation, platform regulation, fact-checking, Digital Services Act, Dead Internet Theory, ChatGPT, DALL-E, Midjourney

=== KEY FINDINGS ===

FINDING 1: Experimental Model Comparison Results
MODEL 1 (Fully Automated AI Workflow):
- Tools: Make framework, OpenAI API, ChatGPT agent, DALL-E, Google Sheets (1000+ quotes)
- Cost: <$20 on OpenAI tokens
- No human intervention
- RESULT: 0 friends on Meta, 0 followers on Instagram
- Technical failures: Instagram suspension (Day 3), Meta glitches, Google Sheets errors, duplicate/missing posts, automation stopped multiple times daily, free trial expiration, empty images, misspelled text, module failures

MODEL 2 (Human-Enhanced AI Workflow):
- Tools: Midjourney with human-curated prompts (iterative refinement)
- Human logged in daily: liked content, added friends, followed random people on Instagram
- Cost: <$20 on Midjourney subscription
- RESULT: 5,000 friends on Meta (maximum), 375 Instagram followers
- Received: "deluge" of compliments, private conversations, calls, voicemails, explicit sexual content, private body part photos, multiple marriage proposals, requests for contact information

FINDING 2: Engagement Comparison Table
Category - Model 1 (Automated) vs Model 2 (Human-Enhanced):
Meta Friends: 0 vs 5,000
Instagram Followers: 0 vs 375
Direct Messages: None vs Deluge (compliments, convos, calls, voicemails)
Explicit Content Received: None vs Yes (private body parts, sexual content)
Marriage Proposals: 0 vs Several
Marketing/Scam Offers: None vs Crypto scams, product promotions, tagging attempts
Image Quality: Artistic but lacked realism vs Photorealistic, consistent persona
Automation Reliability: Frequent breakdowns, glitches, resets vs N/A (manual posting)
Content Consistency: Inconsistent, repeated prompts vs Carefully curated, diverse

FINDING 3: Technical Failures of Pure Automation (Week-by-Week)
Weeks 1-2: Day 3 Instagram suspended; Meta glitch preventing image posts; Instagram blocked for 2 weeks
Weeks 3-4: Google Sheets errors; duplicate posts; missing images; automated flow stopped multiple times daily
Weeks 5-6: Automation became "increasingly glitchy"; stopped running automatically
Weeks 7-8: Free trial expired; empty images; misspelled text on images
Weeks 9-10: Instagram module stopped working; Google Sheets module did not pick up correct data

FINDING 4: AI-Generated Content Dominance on Meta (Nishad, 2025)
- Analyzed 8,885 long-form posts on Meta from 2018 to 2024
- Before 2023: 5.34% of content was AI-generated
- After ChatGPT launch (late 2022): AI-generated content surged
- By November 2024: 41.18% of content was AI-generated
- 4.3x increase in two years
- Average between 2023-2024: 24.05% AI-crafted posts

FINDING 5: Dead Internet Theory
- "Avoiding AI-generated content online has become nearly impossible" (Sahota, 2024)
- AI-created material includes: search results, images, articles, music
- Dead Internet Theory: claim that most content online is produced by AI, not humans
- AI personas lack lived experiences, emotions, and ability to connect authentically (Wheatley, 2025)

FINDING 6: High Engagement Attracts Malicious Actors
Model 2 received:
- Crypto investment scams
- Marketing offers from online social media marketers
- Tagging attempts "counting on users' ignorance to understand profile settings"
- Users potentially exposed to "many tags promoting varieties of scams"
- Explicit sexual content and private pictures
- Marriage proposals and requests for personal contact information

FINDING 7: Meta Monetization Requirements
- Subscriptions: 10,000 followers OR 250+ returning viewers + 50,000 post engagements OR 180,000 watch minutes (past 60 days)
- Subscription fees: $4.99 - $29.99 monthly
- Meta pays $8.75 - $10 per 1,000 views (ActionSprout, 2025)
- Stars program: 500+ followers for 30+ consecutive days, age 18+, no children's content

FINDING 8: Meta Fact-Checking Policy Change (January 7, 2025)
- CEO Mark Zuckerberg announced: fact-checking replaced with "community notes" system
- Meta ended third-party fact-checking partnerships (started 2016 after 2016 U.S. election backlash)
- Meta was "once a pioneer in adopting third-party fact-checking"
- Studies consistently show Meta and Instagram are major sources of misinformation (elections, health topics, hate speech globally)
- Change currently limited to U.S. but has global implications

FINDING 9: Regulatory Approaches
- European Union's Digital Services Act (DSA) now includes provisions specifically addressing synthetic media and manipulation potential
- Requires disclosure of AI-generated content
- Governments worldwide considering legislation requiring disclosure of AI-generated content

FINDING 10: Intentional vs Unintentional Sharing of Fake Content (Ardèvol-Abreu et al., 2020)
- Sharing fake content is a two-dimensional phenomenon: both intentional AND unintentional behaviors
- Some participants expressed distrust toward fact-checkers
- Lack of understanding of how fact-checking process works

FINDING 11: Misinformation Propagation Dynamics (Del Vicario et al., 2016)
- Different narratives (conspiracy theories vs scientific content) spread within similar information consumption patterns
- Conspiracy theories and scientific content are key factors in information consumption
- Developed data-driven model of rumor spreading

FINDING 12: Detection Challenges (Shu et al., 2020; Lazer et al., 2018)
- Weak supervision approach (detecting disinformation with limited labeled data) is a major problem
- User engagement directly leads to spread of misinformation
- Two primary strategies: empower individuals to identify fake content, and platform interventions to limit spread
- Need for more collaboration between social media platforms

FINDING 13: Algorithm + Human Expertise (Collins et al., 2021)
- Combining machine learning algorithms with human expertise enhances accuracy of detecting fake content
- Strategy using mix of algorithms AND human knowledge is most effective

FINDING 14: Social Media Hinders Ethical Decision-Making (Chauhan et al., 2022)
- Social media can influence individual's problem recognition and ethical reasoning
- Overall, social media "tends to hinder ethical decision-making"

FINDING 15: Teen Vulnerability on Social Media
- 76% of students obtain daily news from social media (Riccardelli et al., 2020)
- Teenagers have round-the-clock access to curated, idealized profiles (Woods & Scott, 2016)
- Linked to increased anxiety, low self-esteem, depression
- Teens engage in online sexual activities (OSA) with limited ability to distinguish healthy vs harmful use (Munno et al., 2017)
- Social media can significantly influence teenagers' decision-making for health-risk behaviors (alcohol, tobacco use) (Halpern-Felsher et al., 2016)

=== MEDIA LITERACY TIPS ===

Tip 1: Over 40% of long-form posts on Meta are now AI-generated (November 2024). Nearly half of what you read in long posts on Facebook is NOT written by humans. Assume nothing is human until verified.

Tip 2: Pure automated AI accounts (no human interaction, no replies, no liking other content) get ZERO engagement. If an account does not interact back, it is likely a bot.

Tip 3: High-engagement AI accounts ATTRACT scammers. Successful AI-generated profiles receive crypto scams, marketing exploitation, explicit content, and marriage proposals. Popular accounts are targets for malicious actors trying to exploit followers.

Tip 4: Meta ELIMINATED third-party fact-checking in January 2025, replacing it with "community notes." There is NO professional fact-checking on Meta as of 2025. Users are on their own.

Tip 5: Teenagers are extremely vulnerable: 76% get news from social media, have 24/7 access to AI-generated content designed to maximize engagement, which promotes anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem.

Tip 6: The "Dead Internet Theory" is no longer a theory. Most content online (search results, images, articles, music) is now AI-generated. Users are likely interacting with bots more than humans.

Tip 7: People share fake content BOTH intentionally AND unintentionally (Ardèvol-Abreu et al., 2020). Even people who know something is fake might share it. Do not assume malicious intent - ignorance is equally dangerous.

Tip 8: Conspiracy theories spread using the SAME patterns as scientific content (Del Vicario et al., 2016). Engagement algorithms do not differentiate between truth and lies. If it keeps users on the platform, it gets promoted.

Tip 9: Detecting AI content with limited labeled data is a MAJOR challenge (Shu et al., 2020). Detection tools need massive training datasets. "AI detector" websites are likely inaccurate.

Tip 10: Machine learning + human expertise = best for detection (Collins et al., 2021). Neither alone is sufficient. Use tools AND cross-reference sources.

Tip 11: Social media hinders ethical decision-making (Chauhan et al., 2022). Being on these platforms makes users WORSE at recognizing ethical problems. Take breaks. Do not make important decisions while scrolling.

Tip 12: If a social media account is fully automated (no human interaction, no replies to comments, no liking other content), it is likely a misinformation bot. Pure automation got ZERO engagement in the study. Real human-run accounts engage back.

=== METHODOLOGY NOTES ===

Experiment duration: February 12, 2025 - April 20, 2025 (10 weeks)
Platforms: Instagram and Meta (Facebook)
Content type: Inspirational quotes + AI-generated images

Model 1 tools:
- Make framework (automation)
- OpenAI API (tokenized, paid subscription)
- ChatGPT agent (prompt generation)
- DALL-E (image generation)
- Google Sheets (quote database, 1000+ quotes)

Model 2 tools:
- Midjourney (image generation)
- Manual human posting
- Daily human engagement (liking, following, adding friends)
- Iterative prompt refinement (repeated until satisfactory result)
