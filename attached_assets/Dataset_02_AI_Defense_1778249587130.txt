Source: Nightingale & Farid, PNAS (2022)
Title: AI-synthesized faces are indistinguishable from real faces and more trustworthy
Tags: AI faces, GAN, StyleGAN2, photorealism, uncanny valley, trustworthiness, deepfake detection, perceptual studies

=== KEY FINDINGS (Complete) ===

FINDING 1: Baseline Accuracy (Experiment 1)
- 315 participants
- 48.2% average accuracy (95% CI [47.1%, 49.2%])
- This is BELOW chance performance of 50%
- No response bias detected
- People performed WORSE than random guessing

FINDING 2: Gender x Race Interaction for Real Faces
- Male East Asian faces: higher accuracy than female East Asian faces
- Male White faces: higher accuracy than female White faces
- Not all faces are equally easy/hard to identify

FINDING 3: Gender x Race Interaction for Synthetic Faces
- White faces were the LEAST accurately classified (both male and female)
- Male White faces: less accurate than female White faces
- Reason: White faces are overrepresented in StyleGAN2 training dataset

FINDING 4: Trained Performance (Experiment 2)
- 219 new participants
- With training + trial-by-trial feedback
- Average accuracy: 59.0% (95% CI [57.7%, 60.4%])
- No response bias
- Still far from perfect (41% error rate)

FINDING 5: No Improvement Over Time Despite Feedback
- First 64 faces: 59.3% accuracy
- Second 64 faces: 58.8% accuracy
- Feedback had LIMITED impact
- Some synthetic faces simply have NO perceptible artifacts

FINDING 6: Trustworthiness Ratings (Experiment 3)
- 223 participants
- Scale: 1 (very untrustworthy) to 7 (very trustworthy)
- Real faces average: 4.48
- Synthetic faces average: 4.82
- Synthetic faces are 7.7% MORE trustworthy than real faces
- Difference is statistically significant [t(222) = 13.4, p < 0.001]

FINDING 7: Trustworthiness by Race
- Black faces: rated more trustworthy than South Asian faces
- Otherwise, no significant effect across races

FINDING 8: Trustworthiness by Gender
- Women rated as significantly more trustworthy than men
- Women: 4.94
- Men: 4.36
- Statistically significant [t(222) = 20.1, p < 0.001]

FINDING 9: Most Trustworthy Faces (Top 4)
- Top 3 most trustworthy faces = ALL SYNTHETIC (S)
- 4th place = Real (R)
- See Figure 3 in original paper

FINDING 10: Least Trustworthy Faces (Bottom 4)
- Bottom 4 least trustworthy faces = ALL REAL (R)
- Zero synthetic faces in the least trustworthy category

FINDING 11: Smiling Expression Analysis
- Real faces smiling: 65.5%
- Synthetic faces smiling: 58.8%
- Smiling alone CANNOT explain trustworthiness difference

FINDING 12: Hyperphotorealism Confirmed
- AI faces have crossed the "uncanny valley"
- Findings consistent with recent studies (refs 10, 11)
- Realism extends ACROSS race and gender (not just White males)

FINDING 13: Why Synthetic Faces Seem More Trustworthy
- Synthesized faces look more like "average faces"
- Average faces are statistically deemed more trustworthy
- This is a mathematical/average effect, not intentional design

=== THREATS IDENTIFIED BY RESEARCHERS ===

Threat 1: Nonconsensual intimate imagery (aka "revenge porn")
Threat 2: Small to large-scale financial fraud
Threat 3: Disinformation campaigns
Threat 4: "Liar's dividend" - any inconvenient recording can be called fake

=== RECOMMENDATIONS FROM RESEARCHERS ===

Recommendation 1: Developers should assess if risks outweigh benefits
Recommendation 2: If risks are greater, do NOT develop the technology just because it's possible
Recommendation 3: Parallel development of safeguards (e.g., robust watermarks)
Recommendation 4: Reconsider laissez-faire approach to public code release
Recommendation 5: Develop ethical guidelines for researchers, publishers, and media distributors

=== MEDIA LITERACY TIPS (Direct from research) ===

Tip 1: Human accuracy is only 48-59% - NEVER trust your eyes alone to spot AI faces.

Tip 2: AI faces look MORE trustworthy than real faces. If a face seems unusually kind or trustworthy, that is a RED FLAG, not proof of authenticity.

Tip 3: The top 3 most trustworthy faces in this study were ALL fake. Trustworthiness is NOT a signal of reality.

Tip 4: Training and feedback only help a little (from 48% to 59%). Don't overestimate your ability after "learning" about deepfakes.

Tip 5: Some synthetic faces have ZERO detectable artifacts. No amount of careful looking will reveal them.

Tip 6: White faces are hardest to detect because AI is trained mostly on White faces. Detection difficulty varies by race.

Tip 7: Don't use smiling as proof. 58.8% of AI faces smile - almost the same as real faces (65.5%).

Tip 8: Women's faces (both real and synthetic) are rated as more trustworthy than men's. Be extra cautious with female-presenting faces.

Tip 9: Average-looking faces are more likely to be AI. Real faces have more "imperfections" that actually make them look less trustworthy.

Tip 10: Until watermarks become standard, your only defense is SOURCE VERIFICATION - not visual inspection.

=== METHODOLOGY NOTES (For advanced users) ===

- Used StyleGAN2 (state-of-the-art at time of publication)
- 400 synthetic faces (200 women, 200 men; 100 per race: Black, White, East Asian, South Asian)
- 400 matched real faces from underlying database
- Removed obvious rendering artifacts (makes task HARDER, not easier)
- Amazon Mechanical Turk Master Workers used as participants
- Catch trials included to ensure attention
- Bonferroni correction applied for multiple comparisons
